Find out what that letter wants from you.

Show your AI assistant a letter, bill, contract or form. Paperwork makes it tell you, in plain words, what it says, what you have to do, and by when.

Try it in one minute

Paperwork is a guide your assistant reads before it answers. You don't install anything. You paste it into a chat.

  1. Copy the guide It's a page of plain text. You don't need to read it.
  2. Paste it into a new chat Open the assistant you use and paste it as your first message.
    Open Claude Open ChatGPT Open Gemini
  3. Share your document and ask Send a photo, attach the file, or paste the text. Cover account and ID numbers first if you can. Then ask your question, such as "What is this?"

Tested with Claude so far. Other assistants should work the same way, but nobody has checked them yet. Want it installed for good? See the other ways to add it.

What you can ask

"What is this?" What it is, who sent it, what they want, by when, and any signs it's a scam.
"Is this bill right?" Each charge explained, anything charged twice or that doesn't add up, and what to ask them.
"This is wrong, help me fight it." Your strongest honest case, what proof to gather, and a letter ready to send.
"Should I sign this?" What it really costs, how you get out, and the terms to push back on.
"Help me fill this in." Each box explained, and what to put based on what you've told it.
"I have a pile of these." One list, most urgent first, with the one thing each needs.

Where it stops, on purpose

  • It's not a lawyer, an accountant or a doctor. For court papers, eviction and immigration it explains the paper and the deadline, then points you to free qualified help. It's written to refuse to draft your legal response.
  • It won't work out your tax figures or tell you which box to tick on a visa form.
  • It won't send anything. Letters it writes are drafts for you to check and send.
  • It won't add something untrue to a letter to help you win.

Known limits

  • It can be wrong. Check every date and amount against the paper in your hand before you act.
  • Don't use it in Codex for court papers. There, asked to write a defence, it offers to draft one once you give it the facts. In Claude it declines.
  • Real photos aren't tested yet. The tests so far use typed text.
  • It reports scam warning signs. It can't confirm a letter is genuine.
