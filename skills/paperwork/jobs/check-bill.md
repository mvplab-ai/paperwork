# Job: check-bill

For "is this bill right?", "why is it so high?", "check these charges".

Keep the whole answer under 200 words. This is a limit, not a target: if you are over, cut the least important lines until you are under.

## Blocks, in this order

```
THE SHORT ANSWER
One line: looks right, or what looks wrong and by how much.

PAY BY
The due date and the total as printed. Always include this, even if they asked about one
line only.

WHAT YOU'RE BEING CHARGED FOR
Each charge in a few plain words with its amount. Group small ones.

WHAT LOOKS WRONG
Numbered. Each one: what it is, the amount, why it stands out.
Say "Nothing stood out" if nothing did.

ASK THEM
The exact questions to put to the sender, numbered.

NOT SURE ABOUT
What you could not read, what the bill does not show, what you assumed.
```

## What to check, every time

1. **Add it up yourself.** Add the lines as printed and compare with the printed total.
   If they differ, give both figures and the difference. Recompute any line that shows its
   own sum (units times price, days times rate, a tax percentage).
2. **Duplicates.** The same charge, amount and date appearing more than once.
3. **Charges with no explanation.** Fees with vague names, or anything they would not
   recognise.
4. **Dates.** Charges outside the bill's own period. Two bills covering the same period.
5. **Estimated readings.** Say when a reading is estimated, not actual.
6. **Changes.** If they share an earlier bill, say what changed: price, plan, new lines.

## What you can and cannot say

You can say a bill does not add up, or that a charge appears twice. Those are facts on the
page.

You cannot say a price or rate is normal, fair or too high from the bill alone. If they ask
whether a charge is normal, say what the charge is, say that you cannot judge it from this
bill, and tell them what to compare it with: their contract, last year's bill, the supplier's
published prices.

## If something is wrong

Offer to draft the message asking for it to be corrected. If the bill gives a time limit for
queries, state it.

## Example

```
THE SHORT ANSWER
One charge appears twice. You may have been overcharged £12.00.

PAY BY
£51.90, taken by Direct Debit on or after 2 December 2026.

WHAT YOU'RE BEING CHARGED FOR
Monthly plan £18.00. Roaming pack £12.00, listed twice. Premium calls £3.40.
Device insurance £6.50.

WHAT LOOKS WRONG
1. "Roaming pack - Europe 7 day", £12.00, appears twice with the same date, 03 Nov 2026.

ASK THEM
1. Why is the roaming pack charged twice for 3 November?
2. Will the £12.00 be refunded before the Direct Debit is taken?

NOT SURE ABOUT
Whether you bought two packs on purpose. The bill says to raise questions within 30 days of
18 November 2026.

Want me to draft the message to them?
```
