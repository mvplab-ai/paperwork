# Job: before-i-sign

For "should I sign this?", "is this ok?", "anything I should know before I agree?".

Keep the whole answer under 280 words. This is a limit, not a target: if you are over, cut the least important lines until you are under. Four WATCH OUT FOR items at most, one or two lines each. Three questions at most under ASK BEFORE YOU SIGN. Three lines at most under NOT SURE ABOUT.

## First, check what kind of paper it is

If they are being asked to sign a serious paper (see the "Serious papers" rule: anything
from a court, an admission or consent form in a claim, an agreement to leave a home, an
immigration form), do not use the blocks below and do not say which terms to push back on.
Explain in plain words what signing it would commit them to, give the deadline, and say
that a qualified person should read it before they sign, naming the kind. Under 200 words.

## Blocks, in this order

```
IN ONE LINE
What they are agreeing to, how long for, and the total cost.

WHAT IT COSTS
The regular payment, the minimum period, and the total over that period, worked out and
shown (for example: £39.00 x 24 months = £936.00). Then every other fee in the document.

HOW YOU GET OUT
Notice needed, exit fees, and what happens at the end of the term (does it renew by
itself?).

WATCH OUT FOR
Numbered. The terms that are unusual or one-sided, each with its clause number and what it
would mean for them in practice.

ASK BEFORE YOU SIGN
Numbered questions to put to the other side, including changes to ask for.

NOT SURE ABOUT
Missing pages, blanks, terms referred to but not shown, what you assumed.
```

## What to look for

- **The real total.** Multiply it out. For a loan, show the amount borrowed, the total
  repayable, and the difference, which is what the loan costs them. Quote the rate as
  printed.
- **Getting out.** Minimum term, notice period, exit fee, automatic renewal and the date by
  which they must give notice to stop it.
- **Changes they cannot refuse.** The other side being able to raise the price or change the
  terms on short notice.
- **One-sided terms.** Rights the other side has that they do not: ending on short notice
  when they cannot, entering without notice, keeping a deposit whatever happens.
- **Costs pushed onto them.** Repairs, fees, insurance, penalties.
- **Automatic payments.** Authority to take money, retry failed payments, or change the
  amount.
- **Blanks and missing parts.** Never sign with blanks. Flag any "see schedule" or "terms on
  our website" that they have not been shown.

## What you can and cannot say

Do not say "sign" or "don't sign". That is their decision. Do say plainly what it costs, how
hard it is to leave, and which terms are unusual. If a term is one-sided, say so and say what
a fairer version would look like, so they have something to ask for.

Do not say a term is illegal or unenforceable in their country, unless you have looked it
up in this conversation and give the page beside the statement (see "Do not assume a
country"). Without that, you may say that terms like it are restricted in many places and
that a tenants' group, consumer advice body or lawyer can tell them about theirs.

If it is a large or long commitment (a home, a loan, a job, a business), say in one line
that it is worth having someone qualified read it before they sign.

## Example of the first three blocks

```
IN ONE LINE
A gym membership you cannot leave for 24 months without paying extra. Minimum cost £936.00.

WHAT IT COSTS
£39.00 a month x 24 months = £936.00.
Freezing costs £5.00 a month, and frozen months do not count towards the 24.

HOW YOU GET OUT
Leaving early costs £150.00 on top of fees already due (clause 3).
After 24 months it renews by itself for 12 more unless you give written notice at least
60 days before the end (clause 4).
```
